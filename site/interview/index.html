Basics

Full accounts fresher (B.Com, M.Com) guidePractise a real task

Asked in almost every first interview for an accounts role.

What is a company code?Fresher

The smallest organisational unit for which a complete, self-contained balance sheet and P&L are drawn up for external reporting. It is usually one legal entity, and every FI document is posted in a company code.

What is the difference between a client and a company code?Fresher

The client is the top level of the system with its own master data and settings. Company codes sit inside a client; one client can hold many company codes (legal entities).

What is a reconciliation account?Fresher

A G/L account linked to a sub-ledger (vendors, customers, assets). Every sub-ledger posting updates it automatically and it cannot be posted to directly, so the sub-ledger total always equals the G/L.

What is a chart of accounts?Fresher

The list of G/L accounts that one or more company codes use. Each account has chart-of-accounts data (number, name, balance sheet or P&L type) and company-code data (currency, open item management, tax category).

Which T-code maintains G/L account master data?Fresher

FS00, the central G/L account maintenance (chart-of-accounts and company-code segments together).

What is a posting key? Give examples.Fresher

A 2-digit key on each line that decides debit or credit and the account type. 40 G/L debit, 50 G/L credit, 31 vendor invoice, 21 vendor credit memo, 25 vendor payment, 01 customer invoice, 11 customer credit memo, 15 customer payment.

What is a document type? Give examples.Fresher

A key that classifies a document and controls its number range and allowed account types. KR vendor invoice, KZ vendor payment, DR customer invoice, DZ customer payment, SA G/L journal, WE goods receipt, RE invoice receipt, AA asset posting.

Document date vs posting date vs entry date?Fresher

Document date is the date on the invoice. Posting date decides the fiscal year and period in the books. Entry date is the system date when the document was keyed in.

What is a fiscal year variant? What is V3?Fresher

It defines how many posting periods a year has and how they map to months. V3 is the Indian April to March year with 12 normal and 4 special periods. K4 is the calendar year.

What are special periods used for?Fresher

Periods 13 to 16 take year-end and audit adjustments after period 12, without changing the March monthly figures.

How are posting periods controlled?Fresher

With the posting period variant in OB52. Periods are opened and closed per account type: + all, A assets, D customers, K vendors, M materials, S G/L, with optional account ranges.

What is open item management?Fresher

A G/L account setting where items must be matched and cleared against each other, for example GR/IR, bank clearing or salaries payable. Vendor and customer accounts are always managed on an open item basis.

Parking vs posting a document?Fresher

A parked document is saved but does not update balances; it can be changed, checked and approved. Posting updates the ledger and can no longer be changed except by reversal.

Can you change a posted document?Fresher

Amounts, accounts and dates cannot change. FB02 allows only certain fields such as texts, reference or assignment. A wrong posting is reversed (FB08) and posted again.

What is a cost center?Fresher

A controlling object that collects costs by area of responsibility, for example a department. Expense postings usually need a cost center or another cost object.

What is a profit center?Fresher

An internal unit, for example a product line or region, for which profit is reported for management. In S/4HANA profit center is a field in the Universal Journal line.

What does Simulate do before posting?Fresher

It shows the complete accounting entry, including tax lines and automatic postings, so you can check debit and credit before saving.

Explain debit and credit rules.Fresher

Assets and expenses increase with a debit. Liabilities, equity and income increase with a credit. Every document must balance: total debit equals total credit.

What are tolerance groups?Fresher

Settings that limit the amount a user may post per document or line, and the payment difference a user may accept or write off automatically.

Document currency vs local currency?Fresher

Document currency is the currency of the transaction, for example USD on an import invoice. Local currency is the company code currency (INR). The system stores both and converts at the exchange rate.

You buy goods on credit for Rs. 10,000 plus 18% GST. What is the entry?Guide

Dr Purchases 10,000, Dr Input GST 1,800, Cr Vendor 11,800. The vendor is a liability because you owe the money.

What is the difference between a ledger and a trial balance?Guide

A ledger shows all postings to one account. The trial balance lists the closing balance of every account; total debits must equal total credits.

What is an ERP?Guide

One system where purchasing, sales, inventory and accounts share the same data. When stores post a goods receipt, accounts sees the liability at once without re-entering it.

What is depreciation?Guide

Spreading the cost of a fixed asset over its useful life, because the asset helps earn income for several years.

Why should we hire a fresher like you?Guide

Say what you have practised: for example, that you have posted invoices, payments and a month-end close in a practice ERP and can explain each entry. Concrete practice beats general claims.

Accounts payable

Full Accounts Payable (AP) executive guidePractise a real task

AP interviews test whether you can take a vendor invoice from receipt to payment without paying the wrong amount, paying twice or missing GST credit. Expect questions on the procure-to-pay flow, three-way match and month-end payables.

Explain the accounts payable process.Fresher

Vendor master, invoice entry (MIRO with PO or FB60 without PO), checks (3-way match, duplicate check, approval), payment (F110 or F-53), then vendor reconciliation and ageing in FBL1N.

Which T-codes are used for vendor invoices and credit memos?Fresher

MIRO for PO-based invoices, FB60 for invoices without a PO, FB65 for vendor credit memos without a PO, MIR7 or FV60 to park an invoice.

How do you see a vendor balance?Fresher

FK10N for the balance by period, or FBL1N for the open items that make up the balance.

How does the automatic payment program F110 work?Experienced

Enter parameters (run date, company code, payment methods, next run date, vendors), run the proposal, review and edit it (block or remove items), execute the payment run, then create the payment medium or bank file. Configuration is in FBZP: house banks, payment methods and ranking order.

What are payment terms?Fresher

A key that calculates the due date and any cash discount from the baseline date, for example 2% within 10 days, net 30. It comes from the vendor master and can be changed on the invoice.

What is a payment block?Fresher

A block key on the vendor master or on an invoice that stops the payment run from paying it, for example a manual block or block R from invoice verification differences.

How do you post a vendor advance (down payment)?Experienced

Optionally a down payment request (F-47), then the down payment with a special G/L indicator (F-48), which goes to a separate reconciliation account. When the invoice arrives the advance is cleared against it with F-54.

What is a special G/L indicator?Experienced

A one-character key that posts special transactions such as down payments (A), bills of exchange (W) or guarantees to an alternative reconciliation account, so they are shown separately from normal payables.

How does the duplicate invoice check work?Fresher

If the vendor master flag is set, the system warns when an invoice with the same company code, vendor, currency, reference, invoice date and amount already exists. MIRO has its own duplicate check settings.

How do you do a vendor ageing analysis?Fresher

FBL1N with open items at a key date and the arrears (days overdue) column, sorted or subtotalled into buckets like 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 and above 90 days. Standard due date analysis reports also exist.

How do you reconcile a vendor statement?Experienced

Compare the vendor's statement with FBL1N for the same date. Typical differences: invoices not yet booked, payments in transit, debit notes, TDS deducted, GST differences. List each item, book what is missing, follow up the rest.

What is a one-time vendor?Fresher

A vendor account (one-time account group) used for vendors you buy from rarely. The name, address and bank are entered on each document instead of a separate master record.

How do you reverse a payment that was already cleared?Experienced

Reset the clearing with FBRA and choose reset and reverse. The invoice becomes open again and the payment document is reversed.

What is the MSME 45-day rule and why does AP care?Experienced

Under the MSMED Act, micro and small suppliers must be paid within the agreed period, at most 45 days (15 days if nothing is agreed). Income tax law disallows the expense until paid if the limit is crossed. AP tracks MSME vendors in the master and their ageing.

Which entry is passed when a vendor invoice without PO is posted?Fresher

Dr expense (or asset), Dr input GST, Cr vendor for the gross amount.

What is cash discount and how is it posted?Fresher

A reduction for paying early. If the vendor is paid within the discount period, the payment program takes the discount and posts it to the cash discount received account automatically.

What is the difference between an open item and a cleared item?Fresher

An open item has not yet been matched by a payment or credit memo. Once cleared it carries the clearing document number and date.

Where do you see the vendor balance?Fresher

In FBL1N as the total of the open items, or FK10N. In Fiori: Manage Supplier Line Items.

Which vendor master fields matter most?Fresher

Name, address, GSTIN and PAN, bank details, reconciliation account, payment terms and payment methods. For purchasing: order currency and incoterms.

What is the difference between FB60 and MIRO?Fresher

MIRO is PO-based and matches the goods receipt. FB60 has no PO; the expense is posted directly to a G/L account.

How does F110 work?Fresher

Parameters (company code, payment method, vendors, due date), proposal, edit or block items, payment run, payment medium (bank file).

What is clearing?Fresher

Matching an invoice with its payment. Both items then carry the same clearing document number and date.

What is a three-way match?Guide

The invoice is checked against the purchase order (what was ordered, at what price) and the goods receipt (what actually arrived). The invoice is paid only for quantities received at the agreed price. Any difference goes on hold for the buyer or the store to resolve.

What is GR/IR and why does it have a balance?Guide

GR/IR is a clearing account. The goods receipt credits it and the invoice debits it. A balance means goods arrived without an invoice yet (credit) or an invoice came before the goods (debit). At month-end you review old items and accrue or chase them.

How do you stop a duplicate invoice?Guide

The ERP checks vendor, invoice reference, amount and date. Before posting, search the vendor ledger for the same reference. If a duplicate slips through, block it for payment and reverse it, never just delete it.

When is TDS deducted on a vendor payment?Guide

At the time of credit to the vendor account or payment, whichever is earlier, under the section that applies (for example 194C for contracts, 194J for professional fees). The vendor gets paid the net amount and the TDS goes to the government by the 7th of the next month.

What do you check before releasing a payment run?Guide

Due dates and payment terms, blocked invoices, debit notes to adjust, bank details of new vendors, and that the total ties to the approved proposal.

Accounts receivable

Full Accounts Receivable (AR) executive guidePractise a real task

AR interviews focus on order to cash: raising correct invoices, applying customer receipts to the right items, handling short payments and following up on overdue money.

Explain the accounts receivable process.Fresher

Customer master and credit limit, invoice (VF01 billing or FB70 direct), monitoring in FBL5N, collection and dunning, incoming payment (F-28) with clearing, and handling of short payments, disputes and write-offs.

Partial payment vs residual payment?Fresher

Partial: the invoice stays open and the payment is linked to it. Residual: the invoice is cleared and a new open item is created for the remaining amount, with its own due date.

How do you handle a customer short payment?Fresher

Depending on the amount and reason: write off within tolerance to a payment differences account, keep a residual item, or post a partial payment. Record the reason code so it can be followed up.

What is dunning?Fresher

Sending reminders to customers for overdue items. The dunning procedure defines levels, intervals and letters; the dunning run is F150.

How is customer credit limit managed?Experienced

A credit limit and risk class are kept per customer. Sales orders above the available limit are blocked and released by the credit team in VKM1. In S/4HANA SAP Credit Management uses the BP (UKM_BP) instead of FD32.

How do you post a customer advance?Experienced

Down payment with a special G/L indicator (F-29) to a separate reconciliation account, then clear it against the final invoice with F-39.

What is a payment on account?Fresher

Money received from a customer that is not matched to any invoice yet. It stays as an open credit item on the customer until it is applied.

What is DSO?Fresher

Days sales outstanding: receivables divided by credit sales, multiplied by the number of days in the period. It shows how many days customers take to pay on average.

How is a customer credit memo created?Fresher

Directly in FI with FB75, or from sales: a credit memo request (or returns order) referencing the invoice, then billing creates the credit memo and reduces the receivable.

How do you make a provision for doubtful debts?Experienced

Review the AR ageing and specific risky customers, then book Dr bad debt expense, Cr provision for doubtful debts. Actual write-off of a customer later clears against the provision.

How do you see a customer balance?Fresher

FD10N for balances by period, FBL5N for the open items.

How do you find overdue customer items?Fresher

FBL5N, open items, key date today, and look at the arrears column for positive days. Dunning (F150) runs on these items.

What do you see after posting a customer invoice?Fresher

A new open item in FBL5N (plus), receivables reconciliation account 140000 debited and revenue 400000 credited.

What do you do if a customer short-pays?Fresher

Post the difference as a residual item, a partial payment, or a write-off within tolerance, with a reason code.

A customer paid less than the invoice. What do you do?Guide

Find out why: discount, deduction, TDS or a dispute. Small agreed differences can be written off within the tolerance. Otherwise clear the invoice and leave the difference as a residual item, then follow up.

What is cash application?Guide

Matching each receipt to the invoices it pays so the customer account shows only what is still open. Unmatched receipts sit on account until you know which invoices they cover.

How do you read a debtor ageing report?Guide

It splits open receivables by how long they are overdue, such as 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90 and over 90 days. Older buckets need follow-up first and may need a provision.

Why is TDS deducted by a customer shown as receivable?Guide

The customer pays you the net amount and deposits TDS for you. You claim it against your own tax, so it is an asset (TDS receivable) until it appears in your Form 26AS.

What is a credit memo?Guide

A document that reduces what the customer owes, for a return, a price correction or a discount. It is posted against the customer and matched with the original invoice.

Record to report

Full Record to Report (R2R) and GL accountant guidePractise a real task

R2R interviews test the general ledger and month-end close: journal entries, accruals, reconciliations and a clean trial balance on time.

What is an accrual and when is it reversed?Guide

An accrual records an expense in the month it belongs to when the invoice has not arrived, for example Dr Electricity, Cr Accrued expenses. It is reversed on the first day of the next month so that when the real invoice is posted the cost is not counted twice.

Walk me through a month-end close.Guide

Cut-off for postings, accruals and prepayment releases, depreciation, reconciliations of bank, vendors, customers and GR/IR, intercompany matching, review of the trial balance and variances, then close the period so nothing more is posted.

How do you correct a wrong journal entry?Guide

Reverse it with a reversal document that refers to the original, then post the correct entry. You never edit or delete a posted document because the audit trail must show both.

What is a balance sheet reconciliation?Guide

Proving that a ledger balance is supported by detail, such as a bank statement, a sub-ledger or a schedule. Every difference is explained and old items are cleared.

Why can't you post directly to a reconciliation account?Guide

Accounts like trade payables are updated only from vendor or customer postings so the sub-ledger and the general ledger always agree.

GST and tax

Full Indirect tax and GST executive guidePractise a real task

GST interviews check that you can apply the right tax on invoices, claim input credit correctly and reconcile your books with GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B.

When do you charge IGST instead of CGST and SGST?Guide

When the place of supply is in a different state from the supplier's location (inter-state). Within the same state you charge CGST and SGST in equal halves.

What are the conditions to claim input tax credit?Guide

You hold a valid tax invoice, you have received the goods or services, the supplier has reported it so it appears in your GSTR-2B, and the supplier has paid the tax. Credit is blocked for some items under section 17(5).

How do you reconcile GSTR-2B with your books?Guide

Match each supplier invoice by GSTIN, invoice number and amount. Follow up with suppliers whose invoices are missing in 2B and do not claim credit for them until they appear.

What is reverse charge?Guide

For notified goods and services, or some purchases from unregistered suppliers, the buyer pays the GST to the government instead of the supplier, and can usually claim it back as input credit.

How is a GST rate change handled in an ERP?Guide

A new tax code or a validity date is set up so invoices dated before the change use the old rate and those after use the new one.

FP&A

Full FP&A analyst guidePractise a real task

FP&A interviews mix accounting with analysis: reading the trial balance and P&L, explaining variances against budget, and building a simple forecast.

How do you explain a cost variance?Guide

Split it into price and volume. For example, raw material spend is 10% over budget: 6% because we bought more to support higher sales (volume) and 4% because the price per kg rose (price). Then say what action follows.

What is the difference between budget and forecast?Guide

The budget is fixed at the start of the year. A forecast is updated during the year using actuals so far and the latest view of the remaining months.

How would you build a revenue forecast?Guide

Start from drivers: customers or volume times price, by product or region. Use history for seasonality, add known events, and keep the assumptions visible so they can be challenged.

What are debtor days and why do they matter?Guide

Receivables divided by sales, times the days in the period. Rising debtor days mean cash is stuck with customers even if profit looks fine.

Where do you get actuals from?Guide

The general ledger, usually the trial balance by cost centre or profit centre, after month-end close. Knowing how entries reach the ledger helps you spot wrong postings in the numbers.

Audit

Full Audit and assurance associate guidePractise a real task

Audit interviews ask how you test whether the numbers are right: controls, vouching, sampling and the common ways accounts go wrong.

How would you test for duplicate vendor payments?Guide

Pull the vendor ledger and sort by vendor, invoice reference and amount. Look for the same reference or the same amount on close dates. Check whether the ERP's duplicate check was switched off.

What is cut-off testing?Guide

Checking that transactions near the year-end are recorded in the right period, for example goods received on 31 March are in March purchases and sales invoiced in April are not in March revenue.

What is segregation of duties?Guide

No one person should both create a vendor and approve its payment, or post an entry and approve it. Splitting these steps prevents fraud and errors going unnoticed.

What do you look for in journal entries?Guide

Entries posted at odd times, by unusual users, to rarely used accounts, round amounts, and manual entries to revenue or cash near period end.

Vouching versus verification?Guide

Vouching goes from the books to the documents to prove an entry is real. Verification checks that an asset exists, is owned and is valued correctly.

Treasury

Full Treasury and cash management analyst guidePractise a real task

Treasury interviews cover cash visibility and payments: daily cash position, bank reconciliation, payment runs and managing short-term funds.

What goes into a daily cash position?Guide

Opening bank balances, expected receipts and payments for the day, payroll and tax outflows, and any maturing deposits. The result shows whether to invest surplus or arrange funding.

Why does the bank balance differ from the book balance?Guide

Timing: cheques issued but not presented, deposits not yet credited, and bank charges or interest the bank has posted but you have not. A bank reconciliation explains each difference.

How do you control a payment run?Guide

Only approved and due invoices are proposed, bank details changes are verified, the proposal is reviewed by someone other than the preparer, and the file sent to the bank ties to the approved total.

How do you account for interest on a fixed deposit?Guide

Accrue interest for each month it is earned (Dr Interest receivable, Cr Interest income), even if the bank pays it only at maturity.

What is a cash flow forecast used for?Guide

To see shortfalls weeks ahead so the company can delay payments, collect faster or arrange a loan before it runs out of cash.
