Interview guides / Treasury and cash management analyst

How to prepare for a Treasury and cash management analyst interview

Treasury interviews cover cash visibility and payments: daily cash position, bank reconciliation, payment runs and managing short-term funds.

What interviewers check

  • Daily cash position and cash forecasting
  • Bank reconciliation
  • Vendor payment runs and approvals
  • Working capital and short-term funding
  • Fixed deposits and interest accruals
  • Foreign exchange basics

Common questions, with short answers

What goes into a daily cash position?

Opening bank balances, expected receipts and payments for the day, payroll and tax outflows, and any maturing deposits. The result shows whether to invest surplus or arrange funding.

Why does the bank balance differ from the book balance?

Timing: cheques issued but not presented, deposits not yet credited, and bank charges or interest the bank has posted but you have not. A bank reconciliation explains each difference.

How do you control a payment run?

Only approved and due invoices are proposed, bank details changes are verified, the proposal is reviewed by someone other than the preparer, and the file sent to the bank ties to the approved total.

How do you account for interest on a fixed deposit?

Accrue interest for each month it is earned (Dr Interest receivable, Cr Interest income), even if the bank pays it only at maturity.

What is a cash flow forecast used for?

To see shortfalls weeks ahead so the company can delay payments, collect faster or arrange a loan before it runs out of cash.
